# For The King

A 20-week huddle pathway.

---

## Week 1: Remain in the Vine

Jesus does not call us to produce fruit by trying harder. He calls us to remain in him. The first question of discipleship is not “How much am I doing?” but “Am I remaining with Jesus?”

**Reading:** John 15:1–11

### Discuss

- What does Jesus say is the source of lasting fruit?
- Where are you most tempted to operate independently of Jesus?

### Talking to God

- Jesus, show me what it means to remain in you today.
- Help me notice where I am trying to live by my own strength.
- Produce in me the fruit that only you can produce.

### Journaling

- What usually pulls your attention away from Jesus?
- What would a more abiding week look like in your actual schedule?

### Week's Challenge

- [ ] Read John 15 each day
- [ ] Pray for 5–10 minutes each day
- [ ] Memorize John 15:5
- [ ] Share one place where you need to depend on Jesus with a brother
- [ ] Complete journaling

### Care for the Body

Get outside for 15–20 minutes of morning or midday sunlight at least 4 days this week. Treat your body as something entrusted to you, not something separate from discipleship.

---

## Week 2: Identity Before Performance

Our culture teaches men to prove themselves. The gospel teaches men to receive their identity from Christ and then live from it. We do not obey to become sons; we obey because, in Christ, we have been made sons.

**Reading:** Romans 8:1–17; Colossians 2:6–7

### Discuss

- How does Paul describe the difference between living from the flesh and living by the Spirit?
- Where do you most often look for approval, significance, or identity apart from Christ?

### Talking to God

- Father, help me rest in what you have already given me in Christ.
- Expose the places where I am performing for approval.
- Teach me to obey from sonship rather than striving for it.

### Journaling

- Where do you feel pressure to prove yourself?
- What truth about your identity in Christ do you most need to remember?

### Week's Challenge

- [ ] Write three truths about your identity in Christ
- [ ] Read Romans 8 twice this week
- [ ] Tell one brother where you struggle with performance
- [ ] Pray before one major task each day

### Care for the Body

Choose one nutrition practice for the week: drink water before your first caffeinated drink, eat a protein-rich breakfast, or remove one habitual source of junk food. Choose one, not all three.

---

## Week 3: Lay Aside What Hinders

Abiding is not merely adding more spiritual activity. Sometimes faithfulness means removing what competes for our attention, affection, and obedience. Repentance is not punishment; it is turning back toward the King.

**Reading:** Hebrews 12:1–3; Psalm 1

### Discuss

- What does Hebrews say we must lay aside, and why?
- What distraction, habit, or desire most competes with your attention to Christ right now?

### Talking to God

- King Jesus, reveal what is hindering my faithfulness.
- Give me courage to remove what needs to go.
- Help me replace unhealthy patterns with practices that draw me toward you.

### Journaling

- What habit currently gets more of your attention than it deserves?
- What specific change would make faithfulness easier this week?

### Week's Challenge

- [ ] Name one hindrance
- [ ] Fast from it for 24 hours
- [ ] Replace it with Scripture, prayer, or time with people
- [ ] Tell the group how it went

### Care for the Body

Complete three 30-minute walks, runs, bike rides, or other moderate activities this week. The goal is consistency, not intensity.

---

## Week 4: Abiding in Ordinary Life

Jesus is not only Lord during quiet time. He is King over work, marriage, parenting, money, time, body, friendships, and rest. Abiding means bringing ordinary life under the loving rule of Christ.

**Reading:** Colossians 3:1–17

### Discuss

- How does Paul move from what is true about Christ to ordinary daily behavior?
- Which area of ordinary life is easiest for you to surrender to Jesus, and which is hardest?

### Talking to God

- Jesus, rule over every part of my life.
- Give me wisdom for my work, marriage, parenting, friendships, and responsibilities.
- Show me one ordinary act of obedience I can offer you this week.

### Journaling

- Where is your life most compartmentalized?
- What would it look like to practice the presence of Christ in one ordinary responsibility?

### Week's Challenge

- [ ] Pray over one life area each day
- [ ] Make one practical change in that area
- [ ] Encourage your wife/family or a close friend
- [ ] Share one area where you need prayer with someone else

### Care for the Body

Set a consistent bedtime and wake time for at least 5 nights. Protect 7+ hours of sleep if your season allows.

---

## Week 5: Group Challenge — Move Together

Men often build relationships shoulder-to-shoulder more naturally than face-to-face. Use that strength to build brotherhood without avoiding meaningful conversation.

This week replaces the normal huddle. Pick one challenge together, complete it as a group, and use the time to strengthen brotherhood. Keep the focus on shared effort, encouragement, and enjoying God’s good gifts.

### Choose One

- [ ] Hike or long walk together for 60–90 minutes. Add weighted pack for additional challenge
- [ ] Complete a simple group workout: 3 rounds of 10 push-ups, 15 air squats, 20 walking lunges, and a 400-meter walk/run
- [ ] Play a sport together for 60–90 minutes
- [ ] Do a service project together for 2–3 hours
- [ ] Choose your own active challenge that everyone can reasonably participate in

### Before You Go

- What has God been showing you in the first month?
- What is one area where you want your brothers to help you grow?

### Close in Prayer

Thank God for the men in the group. Pray for strength, perseverance, humility, and joy as you care for your bodies and encourage one another.

---

## Week 6: Known, Not Alone

God does not intend for men to follow Jesus in isolation. Christian brotherhood is not an optional add-on for unusually relational men. We need brothers who know us, encourage us, warn us, and help us persevere.

**Reading:** Hebrews 3:12–13; Ecclesiastes 4:9–12

### Discuss

- What dangers does Hebrews connect to isolation?
- What makes it difficult for you to let other men truly know you?

### Talking to God

- God, thank you for giving me brothers.
- Give me humility to be known and courage to know others.
- Protect this group from superficiality and isolation.

### Journaling

- Where are you most isolated right now?
- What is one step toward deeper brotherhood you can take?

### Week's Challenge

- [ ] Pray for each man in the group
- [ ] Send one genuine encouragement
- [ ] Share one real struggle
- [ ] Invite one man into a deeper conversation

### Care for the Body

Do two strength sessions this week using movements you can perform safely: squat, hinge, push, pull, carry, and core. Keep it moderate and focus on good form.

---

## Week 7: Walk in the Light

Confession is not about being humiliated. It is about refusing to hide. Because Christ has already secured our forgiveness, we can bring sin and weakness into the light and receive grace, prayer, and help.

**Reading:** 1 John 1:5–9; James 5:16

### Discuss

- What does John say happens when we walk in the light?
- What makes honest confession difficult for men, and what does the gospel give us to overcome that fear?

### Talking to God

- Jesus, give me courage to stop hiding.
- Help me confess sin honestly without minimizing or dramatizing it.
- Give my brothers wisdom to respond with grace and truth.

### Journaling

- What are you most tempted to hide?
- What would honest, appropriate confession look like this week?

### Week's Challenge

- [ ] Name one area where you need prayer
- [ ] Confess honestly to a trusted brother
- [ ] Receive prayer without defending yourself
- [ ] Follow through on one step of repentance

### Care for the Body

Take a 24-hour break from alcohol, junk food, social media, or another non-essential indulgence. Use the time to notice your cravings and bring them to God.

---

## Week 8: Truth That Makes Us Stronger

Real brotherhood is more than encouragement. Love is willing to tell the truth, receive correction, and help another man become more like Jesus. Healthy accountability is not policing; it is helping one another stay faithful to the King.

**Reading:** Proverbs 27:17; Ephesians 4:15

### Discuss

- What does biblical truth-telling require from the person speaking and the person receiving?
- Where would you most benefit from honest correction?

### Talking to God

- Lord, make me humble enough to receive correction.
- Give me courage to speak truth with gentleness.
- Help us become men who make one another stronger in Christ.

### Journaling

- How do you normally respond when someone corrects you?
- What blind spot would you be willing to ask a brother about?

### Week's Challenge

- [ ] Ask one brother: “Where do you see a blind spot in me?”
- [ ] Listen without defending yourself
- [ ] Speak one encouraging truth to another man
- [ ] Pray for humility

### Care for the Body

Do a simple mobility routine for 10 minutes on at least 5 days. Include hips, shoulders, back, and ankles. The goal is caring for the body you will steward for decades.

---

## Week 9: Love Your People Well

Abiding in Christ should make us more attentive to the people closest to us. For many men, spiritual maturity is most clearly seen at home—in how we listen, apologize, serve, lead, and love.

**Reading:** Ephesians 5:25–33; Colossians 3:12–21

### Discuss

- What kind of love does Scripture call husbands and fathers to embody?
- Where do the people closest to you most need the fruit of Christ in you?

### Talking to God

- Jesus, make me a man who loves sacrificially.
- Give me patience and tenderness toward my family.
- Help me lead without controlling and serve without needing recognition.

### Journaling

- What do your wife or children experience from you when you are stressed?
- What is one small way you can love your family intentionally this week?

### Week's Challenge

- [ ] Ask your wife/family how you can serve them
- [ ] Apologize where needed
- [ ] Give focused, distraction-free time
- [ ] Pray for your family by name

### Care for the Body

Take your wife/family or a close friend outside for a walk. If possible, make it phone-free.

## Week 10: Group Challenge — Do Hard Things Together

Physical challenge can become a laboratory for perseverance, encouragement, humility, and joy. Choose something difficult enough to require commitment but accessible enough that no man is left behind.

This week replaces the normal huddle. Pick one challenge together, complete it as a group, and use the time to strengthen brotherhood. Keep the focus on shared effort, encouragement, and enjoying God’s good gifts.

### Choose One

- [ ] Outdoor workout and breakfast/coffee together
- [ ] Serve physically together: yard work, moving, cleanup, or another practical project
- [ ] Pick a team sport or recreational tournament

### Before You Go

- Who in the group needs encouragement right now?
- How can we make this challenge about brotherhood rather than comparison?

### Close in Prayer

Thank God for the men in the group. Pray for strength, perseverance, humility, and joy as you care for your bodies and encourage one another.

## Week 11: FOR the King at Work

Work is not separate from discipleship. We work under the authority of Christ, and our faithfulness, honesty, diligence, and treatment of people can display what it means to belong to the King.

**Reading:** Colossians 3:22–24; 1 Thessalonians 4:9–12

### Discuss

- What changes when you remember that you ultimately work for the Lord?
- Where are you most tempted toward laziness, overwork, bitterness, or compromise?

### Talking to God

- King Jesus, receive my work as an offering.
- Give me diligence without making work my identity.
- Help me represent you with integrity and humility.

### Journaling

- What does your work reveal about what you value?
- What one work habit needs to change?

### Week's Challenge

- [ ] Begin each workday with a short prayer
- [ ] Do one difficult task without complaining
- [ ] Encourage a coworker
- [ ] End one workday by thanking God

### Care for the Body

Take three 20–30 minute walks during the workday or after work. Use one walk to pray for people you work with.

## Week 12: A Holy Body

Your body belongs to God. Caring for it is not vanity, and discipline is not legalism. We care for our bodies because they are gifts entrusted to us and because physical habits can either support or hinder faithful obedience.

**Reading:** 1 Corinthians 6:19–20; 9:24–27

### Discuss

- What does Paul mean by saying your body belongs to God?
- How can physical discipline serve spiritual faithfulness without becoming an idol?

### Talking to God

- God, teach me to steward my body without worshiping it.
- Give me discipline where I am passive and freedom where I am obsessive.
- Help me use my strength for your purposes.

### Journaling

- What physical habit most affects your spiritual life?
- What is one realistic change you can sustain?

### Week's Challenge

- [ ] Choose one nutrition goal
- [ ] Exercise 3 times
- [ ] Drink water consistently
- [ ] Avoid one habitual excess

### Care for the Body

Complete three intentional workouts this week, each 20–45 minutes. Modify for your ability, injuries, and season. Consistency matters more than performance.

## Week 13: Fighting for Purity

Purity is not merely avoiding a list of bad behaviors. It is learning to desire what is good and to bring our sexuality, imagination, technology, and relationships under the rule of Christ. Men need truth, grace, and brothers who will not let them hide.

**Reading:** 1 Thessalonians 4:3–8; 2 Timothy 2:22

### Discuss

- What does Paul say holiness requires us to learn?
- What practical boundaries help you flee temptation rather than merely resist it at the last moment?

### Talking to God

- Jesus, make my desires holy.
- Give me courage to flee temptation early.
- Help me receive grace without making excuses and accountability without shame.

### Journaling

- Where are your predictable moments of vulnerability?
- What boundary or replacement habit would make faithfulness easier?

### Week's Challenge

- [ ] Identify one trigger
- [ ] Set one concrete boundary
- [ ] Tell a trusted brother
- [ ] Replace temptation with a healthy action

### Care for the Body

Prioritize sleep and remove screens 30–60 minutes before bed on at least 4 nights. Use that time for prayer, reading, or conversation.

## Week 14: Faithful in What God Entrusted

Men are stewards before they are owners. Time, money, family, influence, gifts, and opportunities belong ultimately to the King. Faithfulness means receiving what God gives and using it for his purposes.

**Reading:** Luke 16:10–13; 1 Peter 4:10–11

- What resource God has entrusted to you most needs intentional stewardship right now?

### Talking to God

- King Jesus, help me hold what I have with open hands.
- Give me wisdom with time, money, influence, and responsibility.
- Show me where I can serve rather than consume.

### Journaling

- Where are you most tempted to act like an owner rather than a steward?
- What one resource can you steward more faithfully this week?

### Week's Challenge

- [ ] Review your calendar
- [ ] Give something away
- [ ] Serve someone without recognition
- [ ] Make one stewardship change

### Care for the Body

Spend one hour doing something active that benefits someone else: yard work, helping a neighbor, serving at church, or another practical act.

## Optional Challenge Pool

These are add-ons, not requirements. A leader can substitute one for a weekly challenge, add one to a month, or let the group choose one together. The goal is embodied faithfulness, not competition.

### Physical

- [ ] Seven days of morning sunlight
- [ ] Seven days of consistent bedtime
- [ ] One week with no sugary drinks
- [ ] One week of drinking water consistently
- [ ] 24-hour technology fast
- [ ] Complete a 5K walk/run
- [ ] Take the stairs whenever practical for one week
- [ ] Seven days of 10-minute mobility

### Spiritual

- [ ] 24-hour fast from food or a normal meal pattern, as appropriate
- [ ] Memorize a Psalm

- [ ] Read one Gospel in a month
- [ ] Spend one hour in extended prayer

### Relational

- [ ] Write a letter of gratitude to your wife or a friend
- [ ] Encourage three men intentionally
- [ ] Ask forgiveness from someone you have wronged
- [ ] Have a phone-free meal with your family
- [ ] Invite a newer man into a meal or activity

### Missional

- [ ] Pray with someone outside your immediate family

### Physical Challenge Guardrails

Physical challenges should build stewardship and brotherhood, not shame. Men should scale challenges to their age, fitness, injuries, health, and season of life. No man should feel pressured to compete, disclose health information, or attempt an activity that is unsafe for him.

## Leader Guide

The leader’s job is not to teach a mini-sermon every week. Your job is to protect the culture, keep men in the Word, ask good questions, model honesty, and help the group move from conversation to obedience.

### Movement 1: Abide

**Watch for:** performance, guilt, inconsistency, and men turning disciplines into scorekeeping.

**Do not force:** long quiet times, emotional disclosure, or identical spiritual rhythms.

### Movement 2: Be Known

**Watch for:** superficial sharing, fear, conflict avoidance, and men trying to fix each other.

**Do not force:** public confession, graphic detail, or immediate vulnerability.

### Movement 3: FOR THE KING

**Watch for:** legalism, overwork, family frustration, purity shame, and physical challenges becoming identity.

**Do not force:** turning bodily stewardship into a competition, or implying that discipline earns God’s favor.

### Movement 4: Make Disciples

**Watch for:** insecurity, perfectionism, and men assuming they need to know everything before investing in someone.

**Do not force:** men into formal leadership before they are ready, or measuring discipleship by visible results.

### A Simple Leader Check-In

- Who seems connected?
- Who seems isolated?
- Who is beginning to open up?
- Who may need a one-on-one conversation?
- What obedience did the group actually practice?
- Who is beginning to invest in another man?

### Final Vision

The win is not that every man completes every box. The win is that men increasingly become the kind of men who live FOR THE KING.

- They abide in Christ.
- They walk in covenantal community.
- They practice honest accountability.
- They steward their bodies, families, work, and influence.
- They make disciples.
- They help those disciples make disciples.

The prayer for the ministry is simple: men who are deeply rooted in Christ, deeply known by brothers, and increasingly willing to spend their lives for the sake of the King.
